Where it started

The first film that made me fall in love with storytelling was The Five Heartbeats.

As a kid I quoted it endlessly, and occasionally got in trouble with my parents for the language. What I was really doing was trying to understand something I couldn't put into words yet: music inside a story doesn't just accompany what's happening. It tells you what to feel before you have time to think about it. As a Black woman and a creative professional, I have carried that lesson into everything I've done since.

Speech-Based Emotion Recognition Using Convolutional Neural Networks

Programmed two machine learning models to detect emotional undertones in speech across English, Spanish, French, Korean, Mandarin, and Japanese, instead of the English-only data that dominates the field. The work reached 85% classification accuracy, a 39% improvement over comparable research, and correctly predicted five of the seven emotions studied. It's where I first asked how sound carries feeling.

Where I'm going

I'm building toward music supervision: deciding what music goes where and why, with cultural intelligence and real intention, and knowing how to clear it.

Longer term, I want to build a production company centered on diaspora storytelling. Black stories, queer stories, and stories that cross borders and generations deserve to be told by people who understand them from the inside, and protected by people who know how to license and steward them correctly. That perspective is what I bring to a team today.

Who is making the decision shapes what gets expressed.
