Re: Feature Brief — "Meridian" (Working Title)
Mood playlist request, Act II — late-night intergenerational scene

A preliminary mood playlist for a film's second act: late-night conversations between a 17-year-old girl of West African and Caribbean descent and her grandmother on a back porch. Slow, intimate, emotionally layered. Music should feel like memory, distance, something loved from far away.

Genres welcome: Afrobeats, Afropop, soul, R&B, acoustic, neo-soul, Afro-folk, ambient with melodic elements. Lyrical or instrumental both welcome. Independent or mid-size label representation preferred for clearance flexibility.

01 — GORGEOUS · Muhsinah

Evokes the feeling of finding safe harbor in someone. Sets the emotional container for the scene, the granddaughter arriving into the grandmother's presence. DC-based independent artist, lyrical, slow tempo. Master and publishing likely independent, favorable clearance profile.

02 — Head in a Jar · Khamari

A sinking feeling as you disappear into deep emotions. Independent R&B, cinematic production. Lyrical but understated enough for dialogue-adjacent placement, works well under scene turns.

03 — Sunny Days · Kennedy Ryon

Bluesy like the past, lyrically illustrates how truth coming to light may not feel good in the moment but is necessary to move forward. Independent singer-songwriter, Americana-soul hybrid, strong lyrical sync potential for scenes of revelation.

04 — Martha · Kennedy Ryon

A story of someone's past illuminating someone else's present. Same independent artist as track 03, clearance efficiency if both are selected. More sparse and intimate, works for quieter dialogue moments.

05 — Some Nights I Dream of Doors · Obongjayar

Sounds like being enveloped in memories, hopes, and fears simultaneously. Nigerian-British independent artist, atmospheric, diasporic production. Lyrical but abstract enough for score-adjacent placement.

06 — Mama Don't Worry · KIRBY

Deep connection to intergenerational themes, honoring sacrifice, learning from the past, forging your own path. Independent soul artist, the emotional peak of the scene sequence.

07 — River · Trevor Jackson

Grief, desperation, and the acknowledgement that choices could have been different. Independent R&B/soul, cinematic production, works for the pivot moment before emotional resolution.

08 — Breathe · Jaz Elise

The release. This conversation changes the trajectory of both characters. Jamaican independent artist, sings in English and Patois, diasporic texture, gentle outro energy for a closing cue.
