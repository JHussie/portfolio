Most Relevant — Music Supervision Coursework

Re-Supervised Scene: Waiting to Exhale — Two Versions

Carnegie Mellon University · Music Supervision · 2024

Re-supervised a scene from Waiting to Exhale with two different music choices — my own selection and an AI-suggested alternative, both edited by me. What the two versions reveal is not just a difference in taste but a difference in perspective. Both tracks express hurt and rage. But a Black woman's hurt and rage expressed through the lens of another Black woman sounds different than when expressed through a lens that does not carry that specific history. Music supervision is a cultural act. Full analysis and both versions available on the Music Supervision page.

Speech-Based Emotional Recognition Using Convolutional Neural Networks

Delaware State University · Honors Thesis · 2022

Built a multilingual speech emotion recognition model using deep learning and CNNs, trained on linguistically diverse data across five languages rather than the English-only datasets that dominate the field. I am Jabreneé Hussie — a PG County native, a violin player, and someone who has spent her entire life trying to understand how stories reach the people they were made for. I came into this industry through information technology, which taught me that the systems underneath creative work are just as important as the work itself. That belief has never left me. Good infrastructure is what makes it possible for a song to find its audience, for a documentary to be built on solid legal ground, for an artist to be paid correctly for what they gave the world. I care about that infrastructure because I care about the people on the other side of it.

I am building toward music supervision — the work of deciding what music goes where and why, of being in the room where those decisions get made with cultural intelligence and genuine intention. And I am building toward a production company centered on diaspora storytelling, because I grew up watching The Five Heartbeats and understanding that Black stories, queer stories, immigrant stories, stories that cross borders and generations — these deserve to be told by people who understand them from the inside, and protected by people who know how to clear, license, and steward them correctly. My machine for giving back to the people who give so much of themselves to their art and their communities is storytelling. That is what all of this is for.
